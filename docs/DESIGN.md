# Keep design

Stalwart Keep is an ephemeral state and messaging server for clustered applications. This document states the consistency claims of the version 1 server and client: what Keep guarantees per operation, under which assumptions, and what it does not guarantee. The wire protocol is [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) and the deployment rules are [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md); a reference such as "PROTOCOL 5.4" or "DEPLOYMENT 5.2" names a section of those documents.

## 1. Consistency claims

This section is the specification the test suites check ([TESTING.md](TESTING.md): the cluster simulator, the fault suite and the Jepsen test). A failed check of a claim is a product bug; behaviour listed under "not claimed" is not.

### 1.1 System model and assumptions

- **Processes.** Servers and clients fail by crashing (including `kill -9` and power loss) or by pausing (`SIGSTOP`, scheduler stalls, VM freezes) for any length of time; a crashed server restarts with no state (Keep keeps nothing on disk). No process is Byzantine.
- **Network.** Messages between any two processes may be lost, delayed, duplicated or reordered (TCP hides duplication and reorder within one connection, not across connections or over UDP heartbeats). Partitions may be symmetric, one-way or flapping, of any length.
- **Clocks.** Every server time reads `CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW`: the hardware oscillator's rate, which no NTP daemon slews (chrony slews `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` and `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` by up to 83,333 ppm by default: up to 8.3% of a hold, 0.42 s of a 5 s lock). Wall-clock steps, slews and jumps (NTP, chrony, `date -s`) do not affect Keep. Assumed: every server's oscillator runs within 833 ppm of real time, so any two differ by at most 1,666 ppm (the TSC clocks of current Azure VMs run within 35 ppm), and a server's clock stands still (a VM frozen with its clock restored, or an OS suspend, which the raw clock does not count) for at most `max_stop` in total within any span of real time as long as the quarantine (default 30 s). The total matters, not each stop: two stops inside one lock's life, or inside one handoff hold, add up. An OS suspend is also measured: the control thread takes the rise of `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` minus `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` as a clock loss on its next turn, and until then a shard drops the replies of any batch that ran a lock operation when its boot clock ran ahead of the raw one, since the control thread's last check, by more than 100 ms plus an eighth of the raw time since that check (about 103 ms). Claim L is the only claim a stale lease breaks, with one exception that stays within K3: an old owner suspended for longer than the leave wait resumes with its lease still valid on the raw clock, and until its next control turn (about 25 ms) its unchecked KV turns can serve a slot that a run-out handoff opened on the new owner (claim K2's no-overlap sentence). Replies a shard forwards to another shard's client are not checked again on that shard, the same residual as a pause after the check. Deployment requirements: a clock source whose raw rate is within 833 ppm of real time, and a peer port (UDP and TCP) reachable only from cluster members. NTP cannot be relied on to reveal one that is not, because chrony corrects far larger errors. keep-server logs its clock source, and logs the kernel's correction whenever it stays above 500 ppm. Checkpoint and restore onto another host is not supported. Beyond `max_stop` the peer-mail clock-loss check is best effort only, and a server stopped that long must be restarted. Process pauses of any length are covered, because the raw clock keeps counting through them. A client that times its own holds needs a clock in the same model; std's `Instant` follows NTP slews.
- **Membership.** Every server has the same member list, peer secret and `max_lock_ttl`; the list changes one member at a time; a restarted server announces a higher incarnation than its earlier runs (the default is the wall-clock second at start). Each cluster has its own peer secret: two clusters sharing a peer secret and node ids would accept each other's heartbeats.
- **Secrets.** Clients hold the cluster secret, never the peer secret, so a client cannot forge heartbeats or peer handshakes.
- **Transport.** In `rpk` and `ca` modes TLS 1.3 authenticates both ends of every client and peer connection, and the session layer then skips the plaintext `Auth` challenge (PROTOCOL 10). TLS runs in the shard loop below the session layer and changes no claim: a shard decrypts everything one receive holds before a single `Service::read`, so frames are parsed and executed as in plaintext mode; it seals a connection's output only after the turn's flush decision, so the output of a turn that is discarded is never encrypted and the record sequence has no hole; and the output caps and Heartbeat skips (section 1.4) count unsealed plaintext plus unsent ciphertext. Handshakes run on the shard threads, and a connection that has not authenticated within 10 s is closed.
- **Start mode.** `--start bootstrap` is used only for a cluster's first bring-up or a restart together with every client; every other start is `rejoin` (the default).
- **Lock keys.** Keys used as locks are in a pinned key class (PROTOCOL 3.8: first bytes 0, 1 and 14 to 17), so the memory cap never evicts them.

Times below are server-side unless they name a client. "TTL" is the TTL of the request; every deadline is rounded up to the next whole engine second, so a key given a TTL lives at least TTL and at most TTL + 1 s after the server executes the request.

### 1.2 Locks and leases

Ops: TryLock, Renew with a token, Unlock (PROTOCOL 3.4). `keep-client`'s `try_lease`, `renew_lease`, `release_lease` and `try_lock` map onto them (PROTOCOL 11 gives one application's mapping).

**Claim L (mutual exclusion).** For each key, define the hold of a token T as follows: it starts when a client receives `Ok` for a TryLock with T, or when it sends a Renew with T that is answered `Ok` (a Renew never creates a lock, so T held the lock from that send until the Renew executed); a Renew's hold starts at its `Ok` instead when a TryLock with T answered `Ok` or with an unknown outcome was in flight at some time between the Renew's send and its `Ok` (that TryLock may have created the lock after the send; an unknown outcome stays in flight). The hold ends at the earliest of (a) the time that client sent that request plus the request's TTL less 833 ppm of it (the clock assumption of section 1.1; 0.5 s of a 600 s TTL), (b) the time it sent an Unlock with T that the server executed or may have executed (an Unlock answered `Busy`, `Moved` or another status that guarantees it did not execute leaves the hold running), and (c) for each TryLock or Renew with T sent after that `Ok` was received and answered `Ok` or with an unknown outcome, its send time plus its TTL less 833 ppm of it (the server replaces the deadline of a lock its token holds, as Redis `SET EX` does, so a renewal with a shorter TTL ends the lock earlier). A later `Ok` for T starts a new hold. Then at no instant of real time do holds of two different tokens overlap.

Claim L holds through every fault of section 1.1: crashes and restarts of any members (the `rejoin` quarantine), pauses of any process, partitions of every shape (the self-fencing lease), and slot moves by handoff (a lock moves with its entry) or without one (quarantine). The client counts a hold from its own send time, so neither network delay nor server queueing can stretch a hold past what the server granted: the server's deadline is at least its execution time plus TTL on its own clock, which in real time is later than the send time plus TTL less 833 ppm of it.

Outside claim L:

- Delete and Set on a lock key remove or replace any holder's token (`keep-client`'s `remove_lock` is a Delete, as on Redis).
- Renew without a token extends any holder's lock (`keep-client`'s `renew_lock`, as Redis `SET EX`).
- Two holders that use the same token are one holder to Keep.
- A lock key in an evictable class can be evicted at the memory cap.

Costs of claim L (availability, not safety):

- After a slot changes owner without a handoff (a crash, a cut-off member), and on every slot of a restarted server (`rejoin`, handed-over slots included), TryLock on an absent key of that slot answers `Conflict` for up to the quarantine (634.031 s by default, Q = `max_lock_ttl` + 1 s + 2 x the self-fence delay + `max_stop` + max(1 s, (2 x `max_lock_ttl` + 2 s + 3 x the self-fence delay + `max_stop`) / 1,199), with the drift term rounded up to the millisecond; the drift term covers an owner's clock and a gainer's clock each 833 ppm off real time over the lock's life and the whole quarantine: a lock death keeps its start across a break in its certificate of up to one self-fence delay, so a flapping link does not re-quarantine the survivors' slots). A slot a joining member gains (a late start, a rejoin, an added member) is lock-quarantined for the full quarantine from the view change, even when its entries arrive by handoff; only slots handed over by a leaving member (`Leave` or a graceful shutdown) whose quarantine had ended when it lost them skip it, because their locks move with the entries, and only from a live hold: a hold lasts at most the smaller of the hold limit and (quarantine - `max_stop`) / 2 on its holder's clock, which a stopped clock can stretch by `max_stop`, so its real life stays below (quarantine + `max_stop`) / 2, and an older one is refused, since a member that gained the slot after the hold began under divergent views may have finished its quarantine and granted. A slot whose quarantine had not ended when its sender lost it (the sender never could grant on it) is handed with its entries but quarantined for the full quarantine from the answer: the sender's end says nothing of a lineage that forked from the sender's own source under divergent views and granted meanwhile, while the receiver, which gained the slot before it pulled, is then as safe as after a gain without data. An answer covers one of the two kinds at a time, so a slot whose sender could grant never inherits the longer quarantine. A slot handed over between placing members keeps the sender's quarantine end, never a shorter one. keep-server refuses lock limits whose hold, (quarantine - `max_stop`) / 2, is below the handoff reply timeout (5 s), so a cluster member needs `max_lock_ttl` of at least 6 s, and it warns when the hold is below the leave wait (10 s), since a leave's hold then ends before its receivers stop waiting.
- Rolling restarts cost lock availability. Every restart quarantines the restarted server's slots (the first bullet), so new locks on the free keys of each restarted server answer `Conflict` for about the quarantine after its restart (634 s by default). A leave within a quarantine of the leaver's own restart, activation or regain hands its slots in the second kind above, so their receivers refuse new locks on free keys until the answer time plus the quarantine. Locks already held move with their entries and keep working in both cases. The quarantine follows `max_lock_ttl`, so setting it to the longest lock TTL the clients use shortens the window (DEPLOYMENT 5.2). Version 1 accepts this cost.
- A lock lost with a crashed server answers `NotFound` to Renew and Unlock with its token; the holder learns it lost the lock at its next renewal.
- A fenced server (one that cannot confirm a majority) and a slot gated by a handoff answer `Busy`; a restarted server serves only after every live member echoed its new life. A member that returns from a lock death still echoing an earlier life of a server makes that server stop serving again until it echoes the new life; the server's slots come back with their entries, and TryLock on their absent keys answers `Conflict` for the quarantine, as after a gain.
- A handoff lengthens a lock's life by the transfer time plus up to 2 s, never shortens it.

### 1.3 KV operations and counters

Ops: Get, Exists, Set (with and without IF_ABSENT), Delete, Incr.

**Claim K1 (per-key linearizability while a slot stays put).** The operations on one key that one server executes while it owns the key's slot are linearizable: each takes effect at one instant between its send and the receipt of its reply, in an order consistent with real time. An operation whose outcome the client does not learn (the connection broke, or the server lost its lease before sending the reply and closed the connection) may or may not have taken effect. When an attempt fails, `keep-client` resets its connection before resending, so on Linux the kernel never delivers that attempt after the resend (elsewhere the reset completes when the client's reader and writer drop the socket, normally right after the failure); the attempt can still take effect after the resend only from bytes that had left the client before the reset, when the path delays or reorders them past the resend or the server executes them more than an attempt timeout after they were sent (a stalled shard or process, including a request read in time and forwarded to a stalled shard), and a stalled owner that lost its slots no longer serves them once its lease lapses. A server that left the client's view listed alive drains, then closes gracefully once nothing awaits a reply and is reset otherwise, at the latest one attempt timeout after the oldest unanswered request was sent or after the view dropped it; one listed dead, or no longer listed, then or in a later view, is reset at once (PROTOCOL 5.4). Expiry happens at one instant in the window of section 1.1 (at least TTL, at most TTL + 1 s after execution); an eviction at the memory cap (below) acts as a Delete at one instant.

**Claim K2 (graceful moves keep acknowledged writes).** When a slot moves by handoff (a member leaves with `Leave` or a graceful shutdown, a restarted member takes its slots back, a member starts late) and the handoff completes within its limits, every entry written and acknowledged before the move is on the new owner with its value, and claim K1 continues across the move. The exception is a value of at least 64 KiB in an evictable class, which is left behind: for that key the move acts as an eviction, a Delete at one instant. Pinned values move at any size. The old owner stops serving the slot at the view that moves it; a leaving server first freezes its shards (keyed requests get `Busy`, which every operation may resend) and announces Leaving only once every shard confirmed, so no turn that executed under the view before the leave ends after the leave's view and loses its replies, unless a shard does not confirm within 1 s (logged): then the leave goes ahead, and that shard's turn in flight at the leave's view can still lose its replies (its connections are drained; safety holds). The new owner answers `Busy` for the slot until it imported the old owner's entries; nothing serves the slot in between. A TTL may grow by the transfer time plus up to 2 s per handoff. The limits: the old owner keeps a moved slot's entries for 30 s, or (quarantine - `max_stop`) / 2 when that is shorter (`max_lock_ttl` below 56 s at the default self-fence delay; the hold), and a slot gained from a leaving member waits at most 10 s for them (the leave wait; a leaving server stops 11 s after it announced Leaving, at most 12 s after the stop signal); a slot whose handoff runs out, or whose old owner crashes during it, opens without the entries, which are then lost as in K3 but with no overlap of owners, except across an OS suspend of the old owner until its next control turn (1.1, Clocks). K2 holds for membership changes made one at a time: a change (a leave, a start or restart, a rejoin after a heal) is in flight from its first action until the cluster is settled (every live member places every other live member, and no slot is gated for an import or held for another member), and the next change starts only after that. Two changes in flight at once are under K3.

**Claim K3 (failover).** When a slot moves without a handoff (its owner crashed, or was cut off from the majority), Keep claims only that a read returns the value of some write of that key, or absent; it never invents a value. In particular:

- A crashed owner's entries are lost: a read on the new owner sees the key absent unless it was written there after the move.
- Between the takeover (225 ms of silence plus up to one heartbeat interval) and the old owner's self-fence (its lease ends 1 s after the last heartbeat round a majority confirmed) both may serve the slot: reads can be stale, and writes and counters can land on either side.
- A cut-off owner that survives keeps the slot in its own view, and its lease lapses. When the cut heals it pulls each of its slots from the member ranked next for it, which took the slot during the cut and now holds it for the returning owner; the slot answers `Busy` until that member answers, at most the leave wait (10 s). A slot covered with data ends with the takers' entries: the returning owner's own entries of that slot are dropped, even when the takers wrote nothing to it, so its counters and tokens restart, consistent with what the majority served. A slot covered empty, or not covered within the leave wait, opens with the returning owner's own entries (its locks re-quarantined), so writes acknowledged by the members that served the slot during the cut are lost, and a key can read as it did before the cut: a deleted key can come back and a counter can fall back. That happens when the next-ranked member holds nothing for it: several members were cut off together, the placing members changed during the cut, the taker is out of the returning owner's reach, the taker revived the returning owner from other members' reports without hearing it itself (a heartbeat-only cut between the two with the peer port up), so its hold records a heartbeat older than the returning owner's regain floor and is refused, or the taker could not hold the slot because a dead member ranked above it was not yet lock-dead.

Claims K1 and K2 hold again for operations that start after the failover settled: the takeover completed, or the cut-off owner serves again.

Counters (Incr) follow K1 to K3. Incr is not idempotent and `keep-client` never resends it after an unknown outcome, so a count is never doubled by the client; across a failover a count can split or restart (a rate-limit window then admits up to its limit again on the new owner).

After an unknown outcome `keep-client` resends only operations whose repetition no other client can observe: reads (Get, Exists, multi-get, Ping) and lock operations that carry a token (TryLock, Renew and Unlock with a token), whose token check absorbs a repeat. Every other write (Set, Delete and their multi-key forms, Renew without a token, Incr, Set with IF_ABSENT, Publish, lock removal by Delete) returns the unknown outcome to the caller: a resend lands after the first execution, so it could take effect a second time after another client's write to the key and undo it, which K1 rules out. DeletePrefix is resent, since it is not ordered with concurrent writes (below). A refusal that guarantees non-execution (`Busy`, `Moved`, no healthy connection) is resent for every operation (PROTOCOL 5.4).

DeletePrefix is not atomic and not ordered with concurrent writes: every key that existed when the delete was ordered and is not written again is gone when the purge ends (PROTOCOL 3.3); a key written while the purge runs, or handed over while it runs, may survive.

Multi-key requests (`key_set_many`, `key_get_many`, `key_unlink_many`) are pipelines of single-key operations: each operation is covered by K1 to K3 on its own, the batch as a whole is not atomic.

Memory cap: every server has one, `--memory` or by default 75% of its memory budget (DEPLOYMENT section 2), split evenly between its shards. Values of at least 8 KiB in evictable classes leave first, oldest written first, while a shard's memory would pass its share. Other entries of evictable classes may disappear before their TTL only once the shard's memory without those values reaches its share. Then a new key in a full segment evicts within that segment only, down to three quarters of it, large values included: non-counters and counters at or below zero first, soonest to expire first, then counters, lowest value first. Past a quarter over the share, the memory a write adds for a value of 9 bytes to 8 KiB, the growth of an overwrite included, is first evicted in that segment, taking only entries that hold such values, never counters or tokens. Keys of pinned classes are never evicted, and writes to them are never refused: they grow past the cap. Writes in evictable classes that cannot make room answer `Full` without executing (PROTOCOL 3.3).

### 1.4 Streams

Ops: Publish, Subscribe, Unsubscribe (PROTOCOL 8). A subscription lives on one connection to one server; streams are not gated by the lease or by placement, so they keep working on a fenced server.

**Claim S1 (order).** The events of one subscription carry consecutive sequence numbers starting at 1 (modulo 2^32). Events published on one connection to one server arrive at each subscription of that server in publish order.

**Claim S2 (no silent loss).** Every event that a server assigned to a subscription (a Publish executed on that server while the subscription is live there, on any of its shards) either arrives, or the subscriber sees the loss: a sequence gap, a Heartbeat or End whose sequence is ahead of the last event it received, or the end of its connection without End. An idle subscription gets a Heartbeat within one heartbeat interval (100 ms), so a loss at the tail shows within that time. A Publish answered `Ok` (confirmed publish) has been assigned to every subscription of its topic on that server.

**Claim S3 (drain barrier).** The End a server sends when it drains a subscription counts every event it assigned to that subscription, on every shard: a subscriber whose last sequence equals that End lost nothing published on that server.

Not claimed for streams:

- End after Unsubscribe is not a barrier: events published on other shards, or in the same receive batch as the Unsubscribe, may be dropped for that subscription without a count, even when their publisher already got `Ok`.
- No delivery across servers: there is no forwarding. Cluster-wide delivery is the client's topology policy, and a subscription that becomes live on a server late (a new member, a moved home, a resubscribe) can miss what was published there before; `keep-client` reports possible loss, and the application runs its catch-up.
- No order across publishers, across servers, or across a drain that moves a publisher to another server.
- No persistence or replay: a subscription sees only events published while it is live; every Subscribe starts at sequence 1.
- No exactly-once delivery: a confirmed publish resent after an unknown outcome can arrive twice. A QUIET publish lost with its connection is not signaled anywhere.

### 1.5 Not claimed anywhere

- Durability: a crash loses every entry and subscription of the crashed server. A graceful restart loses the values of at least 64 KiB in evictable classes that the member's slots held (claim K2).
- Entries of a member cut off from the majority: after the heal, each slot the takers hold for it ends with the takers' entries (K3), so its own pre-cut entries there are gone even when nobody wrote them during the cut (tokens and counters on those slots restart).
- Replication: each slot has one owner; there are no replicas.
- Atomicity or isolation across keys, and order between different keys, even on one connection (forwarded requests may be answered out of order).
- Linearizability across a failover (claim K3), across members that disagree on configuration, or when a membership change starts before the previous one settled.
- Entries across a member-list change: data never crosses member lists, so a list change loses every KV entry and quarantines the moved lock slots, like a full-cluster restart, and a removal leaves a window without a majority between two of its restarts.
- Safety of member lists more than one member apart beyond what servers can observe. Heartbeats go only to members of the sender's own list, so only a member on both lists hears the other one. A server that hears a live peer whose list is more than one member from its own is fenced while it hears it, and it flags that in its heartbeats, which fences its list-mates too (one hop). A list one member longer is decided by either side whenever its sender is not on the receiver's list. So two lists in use never both serve while a member on both lists runs, can decide (its list is not the smaller by exactly one, or it hears a member of the other list that is not on its own) and is heard by its own list-mates. A change that leaves no such member running (disjoint lists, a majority replaced while the shared members are down, a partition that cuts them off, or one-way cuts that leave a shared member on the smaller list hearing only shared members of the larger) makes two clusters that cannot see each other and can split (locks stay covered by the restarted side's `rejoin` quarantine). When a new list's majority forms before the shared member that detects it starts, KV can be served on both sides for about one heartbeat interval plus a check (31 ms). A replayed far heartbeat is ignored once its sender was heard at that life or a newer one. A removed member's captured heartbeats are a different matter: injected over UDP in order (no on-path position is needed), each one is accepted, fences its receiver's whole list through the flag and lapses every lease, so about 5 datagrams a second keep a cluster down and its lock slots re-quarantined. The peer port must therefore be reachable only from cluster members. Version 1 does not rotate the peer secret on a removal. The deployment rule stays one member per list change, with every server `settled true` between changes (DEPLOYMENT 5.3).
- Availability of a server cut off one way inward (it is heard but hears no majority): it fences and answers `Busy` for its slots for the whole cut, because its peers still hear it and never declare it dead, so nobody else takes its slots. No entry is lost and no lock is at risk; clients retry until their deadline, and `fenced` shows in the server's stats line. Unplacing such a server after a bound would trade its entries and a quarantine for availability; it is not in version 1.
- Fencing tokens: a lock grant carries no token the protected resource could check.
- Exactly-once execution under client retries: an operation whose outcome is unknown may or may not have taken effect, and the caller decides whether to repeat it. `keep-client` resends after an unknown outcome only reads, token-carrying lock operations and DeletePrefix (section 1.3), so a resend never undoes another client's write; DeletePrefix is the exception, and it is not ordered with concurrent writes anyway (section 1.3).
- TTL precision beyond whole seconds; expiry happens within the window of section 1.1 plus handoff lengthening.

### 1.6 How the test suites check the claims

The cluster simulator (in process, with a simulated clock and network), the fault suite (real `keep` processes on one Linux host) and the Jepsen test use one fault vocabulary and one set of checkers, which read one JSON line per completed operation on a single test clock ([TESTING.md](TESTING.md)).

Faults: kill(node) (SIGKILL); restart(node) (`rejoin`, higher incarnation); pause(node, ms) (SIGSTOP and SIGCONT); clock-stop(node, s) (a pause whose monotonic clock stands still, as a VM freeze: safe while one server's stopped time within any quarantine-long span stays within `max_stop`; beyond it nothing is claimed); partition(kind) with kinds isolate(node), split (majority against minority), one-way(from, to), peers-only (servers cut from each other, clients reach all), clients(node) (clients cut from one server), bridge (two halves that share one node, which hears both; Jepsen), and heal; flap(kind, period-ms); netem(delay-ms, jitter-ms, loss-pct, reorder-pct); packet(nodes, shape) (netem on the egress of one node, a minority, a majority or every node, with one shape: delay with jitter, loss, duplicate, reorder, corrupt, a rate limit in kbit/s, or a mix of the first five; TCP and UDP checksums and the heartbeat MAC drop corrupted segments, so corrupt acts as loss, and duplicates are dropped by TCP or, for heartbeats, as stale (at or below the sender's last heard incarnation and sequence; foreign beats by the replay floor of PROTOCOL 12.1); packet is checked under the same claims as netem); clock-jump(node, s) (wall clock, no effect claimed); clock-rate(node, ppm) (within the 833 ppm assumption for passing runs); leave(node) (SIGTERM: Leaving, handover, drain); rolling-restart(order); member-add(node) and member-remove(node) (the member-list procedure of DEPLOYMENT 5.3: a removal starts with the leaving member's SIGTERM, then every remaining member restarts with `rejoin` and the new list, one at a time); kill-group(nodes, stagger-ms) (SIGKILL of two members, or of a majority, one right after another; after the hold each restarts with `rejoin`, one at a time, each once the previous one is ready plus stagger-ms; while a majority is down the survivors are fenced, so no request sent at or after the last kill plus the self-fence delay plus 600 ms (at least one heartbeat interval plus the client's in-flight bound) and answered before the first restart's process start may carry a command result: only `Busy`, `Unavailable`, `GoAway`, `Moved`, the client's deadline and transport errors pass, and a run with no such request fails); overlap(partition, hit, lead-ms, ends) (an isolate or a majority against minority split, then after lead-ms a kill or a pause of a member on the majority side; the two end lead-ms apart in the drawn order: partition first, so the windows cross, or hit first, so the hit lies inside the partition).

- Locks (L): every client logs, on one test clock, the send time, reply time and TTL of each TryLock, Renew and Unlock with its token; the checker builds the holds of claim L and reports any overlap of two tokens on one key. It runs under every fault of section 1.1, including `kill -9`, `SIGSTOP`, partitions (symmetric, one-way, flapping), `tc netem` delay, loss and reorder, and rolling restarts. Clock faults: wall-clock jumps on servers (must have no effect); boot-clock rate skew only within the 1,666 ppm assumption.
- KV (K1, K2): per-key linearizability (Knossos, or Elle's register and counter models) over histories in which no slot changes owner without a handoff: graceful leaves, rejoins and late starts are included; crashes and cut-off members are not. Unknown outcomes are recorded as indeterminate; the checked keys stay below the memory cap, and TTLs are either absent or long against the run so expiry does not enter the model. The same suites also run with a low memory cap and a writer of large values on keys of its own, which no history records: the checked keys must stay linearizable through the pressure.
- KV (K3): under crashes and partitions the checker verifies only what K3 allows: every read returns a value some write of that key produced, or absent; and the operations between two failovers form a linearizable per-key history when that segment may start from any value a write produced before it, or absent. A failover window ends a key's segment only when the window may move that key's slot without a handoff. With an owner map (the fault suite writes one per KV case, and the simulator builds the same map from its starting member list), a window restarts a key when one of these holds:
  - it hit no single member: a split, netem or flap without one node, a crossed leave, or a crossed change without a node;
  - it is a run-out and the key's owner was unavailable when it started (a run-out moves slots whose owner is away). A run-out restarts every key if a window without a node is open when it starts, or if nobody is unavailable then;
  - it hit the key's owner in the full placement;
  - it started while that owner was unavailable, because the member it hit may then hold the slot. The owner is unavailable from the start of any failover or membership window on it until that window ends, extended by the settle time; for a kill or leave this is the span of its kill or leave record, which lasts until the member is back and settled, or to the end of the run.

  A window on another member leaves the key under K1 and K2, so a loss on a member that was not faulted is reported. A run without an owner map, a key missing from it, and any run whose member list changed (member-add, member-remove, the simulator's list suites) keep the suite-wide rule, in which every window restarts every key; the checker's report names the rule it used. The owner map counts only when the checker reports 0 keys without owner. A member that never returns leaves its own keys unchecked from its kill to the end of the run; under the suite-wide rule, where overlapping windows merge, nothing after such a kill is checked. Only scripted schedules do that: the random plans restart every killed member, and the fault suite restarts every member it kills.

  The simulator opens a window with each fault; a new failover or membership window on a member starts closing that member's earlier windows, except kill, leave, pause and freeze windows. A heal, a skewed heal or a calm starts closing every open window except kill and leave windows, which close when that member's rejoin settles, and pause and freeze windows, which start closing when the member resumes. A closing window ends once every running member is settled, apart from members that are still inside an open failover or membership window of their own; while a window without a node is open, nobody is left out. A window still open at the end of the run ends with it. A relist writes a member-add or member-remove record, so the history alone shows a list change. The skewed heal (node, late, delay, with delay at most 50 ms) heals every link and delays the messages from `node` to `late` until the next heal or calm. It opens no window, because it cannot move a slot by itself.
